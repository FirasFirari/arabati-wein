Support Arabati Wein

عربتي وين؟ means “where is my car?”. It is a free, scam-free public registry of vehicles looted or lost in the Sudan war, live at mycarsd.online. One Sudanese family built it while searching for their own car. This page sets out what it would take to run it for every family, and what each level of funding adds.

ملخّص بالعربية

«عربتي وين؟» سجلٌّ عام مجاني للعربات المنهوبة والمفقودة في حرب السودان، على الرابط mycarsd.online. يجمع كشوفات العربات التي تنشرها لجان الشرطة والمتطوعون والمجموعات العامة، نصاً وصوراً، ويحوّلها إلى سجل واحد لكل عربة يمكن البحث فيه برقم الشاسي أو اللوحة بالأرقام العربية أو الإنجليزية. من لا يجد عربته يسجّل رقمها، ويُفحص الرقم كل ساعة مقابل كل منشور جديد، ويُبلَّغ صاحبه إن ظهر. كل صفحة تُرشد إلى الطرق الرسمية المجانية (البلاغ الإلكتروني في sudanpolice.net وإدارة المرور) وتحذّر من النصب: لا يوجد رقم واتساب رسمي، ولا رسوم للاسترداد، ولا ترسل رقم شاسيك في الخاص لأحد.

المشروع بناه أفراد أسرة سودانية واحدة أثناء بحثهم عن عربتهم، ويعمل حتى اليوم بلا تمويل على حاسوب منزلي واحد. هذه الصفحة تبيّن ما يلزم لتشغيله لكل الأسر: أولاً خادم دائم وتنبيهات آلية (٣٠٠٠ دولار)، ثم معالجة يومية وروبوتات واتساب وتيليجرام (١٠ آلاف)، ثم منسّق مجتمعي ودليل الأحواش الرسمية (٢٥ ألفاً)، ثم مؤسسة مسجَّلة تتعاون مع شرطة السودان وإدارة المرور (٦٠ ألفاً). المبادئ لا تتغير بالمال: مجاني دائماً، التنبيه برقم مطابق لا بالشكل، لا تواصل مع البائعين، والأرقام تُنشر بصدق بما فيها صفر استردادات حتى الآن.

Why this exists

When Khartoum emptied in 2023, cars were taken by the hundreds of thousands. They are now found in police yards, photographed by field volunteers, listed by recovery committees and offered for sale in dealer groups, in lists that are posted as photos of handwritten pages, scattered across dozens of Facebook groups and Telegram channels. A family that still holds its chassis number has nowhere to type it.

Into that gap came extortion. Fake “recovery committees” ask for the chassis number in private, demand fees, hijack WhatsApp accounts through the six-digit code, then vanish. The registry answers both problems: one place to search, and a clear route to the official free channels, with a scam warning beside every contact number it shows.

The demand is documented by the state. Sudan Police’s electronic reporting platform logged 107,656 reports in June 2026 alone, 78,464 of them missing vehicles, according to the police spokesman’s public statement. Khartoum State committees hold more than 8,000 collected vehicles at 38 sites. Owners are told to come and search by chassis number.

Who runs it

A Sudanese family, as volunteers, with a personal AI subscription and under US$200 of direct costs since August 2026. There is no legal entity yet. The code is published under the Apache 2.0 licence so the project can be audited, contributed to, and run by others; the policies it follows are public (privacy, safeguarding) and do not change with money.

Built and working

  • Arabic-first search with English toggle, by chassis, plate, make, model or year, in Arabic or Latin digits.
  • Collectors for Telegram channels and public Facebook recovery groups; a Telegram bot families forward posts to.
  • A parser for the long handwritten كشف lists, plus OCR of every photo, with a grounding gate that deletes any identifier not present in the source. Over a hundred invented numbers were caught this way; none were self-reported by the models.
  • Alerts only on an exact identifier, never on resemblance. A white 2015 Corolla looks like every other one.
  • Standing watches: a family that finds nothing leaves a number and is checked against every new list, hourly.
  • Official free channels and the scam warning on every page, including the “no official WhatsApp number exists” rule.
  • Visitors from Sudan, Egypt, the Gulf and the diaspora, almost all on phones. AI assistants have begun recommending the site.

Not yet, and why it matters

  • It runs on one home computer. When the computer is off or short of memory, nothing is collected and no watch is checked. It was off for three days in October.
  • Lead relay is still by hand. A matched watch alerts the operator and prepares the message; a person still sends it.
  • Intake outruns processing. Three to five thousand posts arrive a day; the free processing tier reads about eight hundred. The backlog grows daily.
  • Lists are scraped, not sent. The sustainable model is recovery groups, field platforms and police committees sending their lists directly.
  • No messaging-app access. Sudan visits the website rarely; Sudan lives on WhatsApp and Telegram.
  • No legal entity. Nobody can receive a grant, sign a data-sharing agreement, or take over if the founders cannot continue.

What funding will not do

It will not recover a specific car, and it will never charge a family or contact a seller. The registry tells a family where a number has appeared and sends them to the police, the Traffic Department and the official yards. Approaching a holder directly is how families get extorted, and the project will not do it or help anyone do it.

We also do not promise matches. A chassis search can fail when a looted car has been given a wrecked car’s stamped parts, which is the standard laundering method. That is why photo matching and official data sharing are on the ladder.

Principles that do not change with money

  • Free, always. No fee to search, register a watch or receive a lead.
  • Identifiers, not resemblance. An alert needs a chassis or plate. Scores never trigger one.
  • No private channel. We never ask for a chassis number in a direct message, and we say so on every page.
  • Documents stay private. Text read from a photographed registration card is never published.
  • Every record names its source and links to the original post where one exists.
  • Numbers are published honestly, including zero recoveries, until that changes.

Questions funders and families ask

Is Arabati Wein free to use?

Yes, always. There is no fee to search, to register a standing watch, or to receive a lead. Official vehicle recovery in Sudan is also free; anyone asking for money to release a car is not the police.

Will the registry recover my car?

No. It tells you where your chassis or plate number has appeared in public posts and sends you to the official channels: the free report at sudanpolice.net/reg/car_stealing and the Traffic Department in person. It never contacts sellers or holders.

Do you contact the person who posted my car?

Never, and we ask families not to either. Approaching a holder directly is how families get extorted and how a car disappears again. The identifier and an official report are what settle it.

What do you store about me?

If you register a watch: the number you typed, your contact and your note, used only to tell you when the number appears. Never published. The site records search queries in analytics; see the privacy page. Photos and text read from photographed documents are never published.

How are the numbers in the registry verified?

Every identifier must be present in the source post’s text or in the OCR of its photos, or it is deleted. Alerts need an exact chassis or plate match; resemblance is never enough. Each record links to the original post so you can check it yourself.
