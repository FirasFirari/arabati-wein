## PUT ONLY THE IDENTIFIER IN THE IDENTIFIER FIELD

`chassis` and `plate` must contain the identifier and nothing else. No
parentheses, no caveats, no comparisons, no "from text", no "does not match".
Every observation, doubt, source note and comparison belongs in `description`.

This is not a style preference. Identifiers are normalised by stripping
non-alphanumeric characters, so commentary is folded into the value. An agent
wrote:

    chassis: "5223949 (from text; does not match target VIN fragment {{CHASSIS_TAIL}})"

which normalised to ...FRAGMENT{{CHASSIS_TAIL}} and registered as a MATCH on {{CHASSIS_TAIL}} —
a phantom hit on the exact number this project exists to find. The mirror of
that failure is worse: a REAL match arriving with prose appended would have a
normalised value no exact-match query can find, and the car would be missed.

If you are unsure of a character, put the legible characters in the field and
explain the uncertainty in `description`.

## DO NOT TRANSCRIBE OWNER NAMES OR ADDRESSES FROM OFFICIAL DOCUMENTS

Photographed vehicle-registration cards (رخصة مركبة / استمارة) carry the
registered owner's full name, home address and personal phone. Record the
VEHICLE identifiers from them — VIN, plate, make, model, year — and write
`from a vehicle registration document photo` in `description`. Do NOT copy the
owner's name, address, or the phone printed on the card.

Why: this registry is published on a public web page. A poster who writes
"call me on 09xx" has chosen to be contactable. The owner of a registration
card that a stranger photographed and posted has not, and tying a named person
to a home address and a vehicle on an indexed page — during a war — can put
them in danger. A phone number offered in the post body is fine; one lifted off
a government document is not.

The exception is the phone number the POSTER gives for contact about the
vehicle. That is the contact field's purpose and it stays.
